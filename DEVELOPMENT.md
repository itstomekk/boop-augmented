## Rules as implemented

Source: the official boop the Halls! rules on Dized (publisher-verified) plus the Dized FAQ. 2-4 players: seats rotate 0 to n-1, pools and captured counts are per seat, a pounce scores for the cat's owner, and only the player on turn graduates a line (house rule: the printed games are for two).

1. Board: `levels` L (4 to 8), grid N = 2L-1. Tier of a cell = Chebyshev ring from the centre (ring 0 = treetop). The 4 grid corners are missing (cut-off base corners). Off-board cells count as off the tree.
2. Each player: `pieces` kittens (default 8) and 3 ornaments. Cats come only from graduation, so a player always has `pieces` cats+kittens.
3. Turn start: no own ornament on the tree and one in the pool -> phase `ornament`: hang it on a free ring-1 cell, else the next free ring outward. Ornaments do not boop.
4. Phase `place`: put a kitten or cat on any free cell. Each adjacent piece moves one step outward along its own ray if the target is free; no chain reactions. Kittens do not move cats.
5. Ornaments never move to a smaller ring. An ornament pushed off the tree: own -> pool, opponent's -> captured by the active player.
6. A cat pushed onto an ornament (pounce): both leave; the cat returns to its owner's pool; the ornament is captured by the cat's owner, or returned if it is theirs. A kitten pushed onto an ornament is blocked.
7. After all movement: 3 captured ornaments -> naughty win (active player checked first). Only the active player's lines count (FAQ: the opponent's lines do not score on your turn). An all-cat line -> nice win. All `pieces` on the tree and all cats -> nice win. One line -> graduates automatically. Several -> phase `chooseLine`. No line and all pieces on the tree -> phase `promote` (remove one own kitten/cat; it returns as a cat; then the turn passes).

Simplifications: when a turn offers both a line and the all-on-the-tree graduation, the line wins (the printed rules let the player choose). The tier geometry (7x7, rings of 1/8/16/20 cells, cut-off corners) was read from setup photos and video frames, not from a printed diagram.

### Square boards (`shape: 'square'`)

`newGame({shape: 'square', size: 6..10, players, pieces, ghost})`. `state.L` and `state.N` both equal the side length, there is no tier structure (`geometry(size, 'square')` has all `N*N` cells and a distance-from-middle `ring` used only by the CPU), and `ornaments` is 0, so there is no ornament phase and no naughty win. Every other rule is the tree's: boop in 8 directions, kittens do not boop cats, lines graduate, all-on-the-board promotion, three cats in a row or all-cats wins. 6×6 is the printed boop board; 7-10 are house sizes (Auto kittens: 8 + 2 per extra row). `maxPieces(levels, players, ornActive, shape)` caps the kittens so a free cell always remains. `balance_sim.js` takes `SHAPE=square [GHOST=1] [PLAYERS=n]`: 1-ply CPU self-play (10 games per size and kitten count, 8 for 4 players, 400-ply cap): all games ended except two 6×6 games at 12 and 16 kittens; 32-65 plies on 2 players, 69-105 on 4 players, always with the nice (cats) win.

### Ghost cats (`ghost: true`, square only)

A house adaptation of BOOoop.'s ghost cats (publisher's Dized rules page and video walkthroughs; the retrievable text of that page was cut off and the rulebook PDF text could not be extracted, so rare edge cases may differ).

- State: `ghosts[p] = {on:false}` or `{on:true, d, a, t}`. A **stitch** is the seam between two neighbouring cells: `d` is the travel direction (0 down, 1 up, 2 right, 3 left), `a` the seam index across the lane (0..N-2), `t` the position along it (row for d 0/1, column for d 2/3). Entry stitches have id `d*(N-1)+a` (`4*(N-1)` of them, `stitchFromId`); `ghostCells` gives the two cells beside a stitch, `ghostPlace` its drawing position. Opposite directions on one seam share stitches.
- Phase `ghost` follows the piece placement (after its boops) when the player's ghost is in the pool, a free entry stitch exists and the ghost did not leave the board this turn. `placeGhost(s, id)` or `skipGhost(s)` ends it; neither moves anything. Ghosts do nothing on the turn they are placed.
- In `play()`, after the piece's boops, the player's ghost on the board moves one stitch (`moveGhost`): stitches already holding another ghost are skipped (`passed`), past the last stitch it returns to the pool. `scareAt` then moves each piece beside the stitch one cell sideways, jumping over occupied cells to the first free one, or off the board (back to its owner). Events carry `ghost`, `leap`, `from`, `to`, `tr`, `tc` for the UI.
- Only then `resolveAfterMove` runs: lines/wins/promotion for the player on turn. Lines a ghost makes for others wait until that player's own turn, as in the published rule. On an `N×N` board a ghost placed on turn 0 moves on the owner's next `N-1` turns and leaves on the `N`th (6×6: five moves, then out on the sixth turn after placement, as in the published game).
- CPU: `bestGhost` replays each free stitch's whole journey over the current board and places where the evaluation gains most (keeps the ghost otherwise); turn search skips the ghost phase (`autoResolve`) and assumes other pieces stay put, so it is a heuristic, not a search.

### Boopmas spirits (`boopmas: true`, tree only)

A house adaptation of the **Ghosts of Boopmas!** expansion, built from the publisher's product text, a review and public descriptions; the printed rulebook was not available, so timing and edge cases are decisions of this project (listed below). State: `spirits = {holder: null | seat, used: [bool, bool, bool]}` (`SPIRITS` = Psst Psst, Yet to Pounce, Jacob Meowly). `newGame` throws for `boopmas` on a square.

- Hand-over: `graduateLine` (any line with at least one kitten, so also mixed lines and `chooseLine`) and `promote` of a **kitten** call `handOver`: `holder = (turn + 1) % players`, `used` all false. A cat promotion or a three-cat win does nothing. The holder's own graduation moves the board on (reset). Nobody holds it before the first graduation.
- `play(s, idx, isCat, {g, d})`: `g` = spirit 0-2, `d` = direction 0-7 (index into the eight directions, `DIRS8` order NW, N, NE, W, E, SW, S, SE) for Meowly only. Throws when the seat is not the holder, the spirit is spent, or arguments are inconsistent. The spirit is marked used before `resolveAfterMove`, so a graduating turn resets it. `legalSpirits(s, idx)` lists `{g}` / `{g: 2, d}` for a placement; Meowly directions are only those with a piece in them. `legalMoves` stays plain.
- Psst Psst boops only the diagonal directions (indices 0, 2, 5, 7), Yet to Pounce only the straight ones (1, 3, 4, 6); the kitten-cannot-boop-cat rule stays. Meowly boops only direction `d`, with `anyKind` (a kitten boops a cat) and `leap`.
- Leap (`boopPiece`): a piece whose target cell is occupied jumps over the contiguous occupied cells to the first free cell, or leaves the board when the line reaches the edge (it goes home; an ornament is captured/returned as usual). A cat pushed onto an ornament still pounces (checked first); a kitten pushed onto an ornament jumps. Ornaments never go to a smaller ring: the first target is checked (the ring rule is not a blocker that can be jumped) and so is the landing cell; if the landing cell is higher up the ornament stays.
- Events carry `leap: true` for the UI hop. `placed` carries `g` and `d`.
- Choices that are not in the published rules: next seat, one spirit per turn, reset at every graduation (including kitten promotions), the direction is chosen by the player, jumping off the tree, interaction with ornaments, 3-4 players.
- CPU: `turnCandidates(s, maxOrn, powers)` adds every legal spirit to every placement at the **root** of `bestMove` only (replies use plain moves, to keep it fast).

### Shuffle powers (`shuffle: true`, square only)

A house adaptation of **boop. Shuffle** (a card game) built from the publisher text, a written review and a video walkthrough: **no deck, no draws, no hidden information**; the powers are pieces a player chooses. Excludes ghosts (`newGame` throws), needs `pieces >= 6` (`SHUFFLE_MIN_PIECES`), and `maxPieces(.., shuffle)` subtracts the two wild pieces' cells.

- Pool per seat: `{k: pieces-2, c: 0, o: 0, ks: 1, ka: 1, cs: 0, b: 1}`; shared `wild = {k: 2, c: 0}` (`WILD_PIECES`). A piece is `{id, p, kind: 'k'|'c', x?: 'w'|'s'|'a', cov?: {p}}`; `poolKey(q)` gives the pool key (`k`, `ks`, `ka`, `c`, `cs`). `p === -1` is a wild piece: it returns to `wild[kind]` instead of a pool, counts as the owner's piece in `findLines` for whoever is on turn (`mine(i)` accepts `x === 'w'`), and is never in `onBoardCount` (so it cannot be promoted or count for the all-cats win).
- `play(s, idx, isCat, {x})`: `x` is `'w'`, `'s'`, `'a'` (kittens only) or `'b'` (the blankie, `isCat` must be false, `idx` must hold an awake piece). `legalMoves` lists them as `{idx, cat, x}` entries.
- Super: `steps = 2` (the second cell only if the first is free; off the board after one cell is off). A super **cat** also has `leap`: when the first cell is occupied it jumps over the contiguous pieces. Super kittens never leap.
- Scaredy (`scareScaredy`, after the placement's boops): a queue seeded with every event that ended on a cell; for each landing, every awake `x: 'a'` kitten in the 8 neighbours (not the mover, not already moved this placement, not asleep) is booped one cell in the mover's direction; a successful move re-enters the queue. Events carry `scare: true`. Each scaredy moves at most once per placement, which also guarantees termination.
- Blankie: `cov: {p}` on the piece (pieces are replaced, never mutated, because states share piece objects). Placing it also boops like a kitten (`boopNeighbours` with the blankie as a kitten-like booper). `findLines` ignores sleepers and `onBoardCount().cats` excludes them; `total` includes them. `blanketBoop`: booping an eligible sleeper removes the blanket, the piece stays, the blanket moves one cell in the boop direction onto an awake piece (it falls asleep, owner `cov.p` unchanged) or goes back to `pool[owner].b`; no loose blankets on the board. Event `{result: 'blanket', blanketOwner, blanketTo, blanketCovers}`. `promote` of a sleeper returns the blanket.
- Graduation (`graduateLine`/`promote`): kitten and scaredy become `c`, a super becomes `cs`, a wild piece becomes `wild.c++`.
- Choices that are not in the printed game (see README): chosen not drawn pieces; counts (1 super kitten, 1 scaredy kitten, 1 blankie per seat, 2 shared wild kittens); no adjacency requirement and no floating grid; blankie boops like a kitten; no loose blankets; super cat leaps only when its first neighbour is blocked; 2-4 players, 6-10 squares.
- CPU: `legalMoves` already lists every power, so `turnCandidates` evaluates them at both search levels (about 4x the candidates of a plain square; measured below).

## 3D view

Pure CSS 3D, no WebGL. Power badges (`data-x`) and blankies (`.blanket`) are children of the piece box and billboard with it. The square board is a single slab (`.slab` with four walls); ghosts are billboards like the pieces, and the entry stitches are buttons lifted onto the slab. The board gets `scale3d(zoom, zoom, zoom) rotateX(tilt) rotateZ(spin)` inside a `perspective` wrapper; the cell size is chosen so the board fits at any spin and tilt (diagonal vs tower height). Each tier's top face sits at `translateZ((L - ring) * 0.42 * cell)`, side walls are rotated divs, and pieces are billboards (`rotateZ(-spin) rotateX(-tilt)`) so they always face the camera. Cells stay real buttons, so clicks and keyboard focus work the same in 2D and 3D.

The camera now uses uniform `scale3d(zoom, zoom, zoom)`: unlike CSS `scale()`, it scales Z too. The sky reads the tree wrapper's computed CSS perspective (currently 1900px) and perspective-origin (currently 50% 35%), converts that origin to viewport coordinates, and projects seeded world directions with the same `Rx(tilt) Rz(spin)` orientation. Uniform scaling cancels for points at infinity, so zoom leaves distant stars fixed. Resize, scroll, board rebuilds and camera input repaint the sky. `test/sky_camera_test.py` checks recorded canvas positions against both analytical CSS intrinsics and actual CSS-transformed far-world points in Firefox and Chromium, including mobile portrait/landscape and zoom extremes.

## Kitten count on bigger trees

`test/balance_sim.js` plays fast 1-ply CPU-vs-CPU games for every tree size and kitten count. With 8 kittens a game lasts about the same number of turns on every tree, but the pieces are spread thin on the big ones. Auto adds 2 kittens per extra tier, so a game on 8 tiers with 16 kittens lasts about 46 plies (23 turns each), close to the classic tree at its official 8. Above 16 games get long and the all-cats-on-the-tree win becomes unrealistic. On 5+ tiers the naughty (ornament) win almost disappeared with the official ornament rule: ornaments hang near the centre, far from the edge. More ornaments alone did not fix it; more ornaments hung nearer the edge did (6 tiers, 3 at once on ring L-3: 5 of 8 CPU games won naughty). Auto ornaments (`ornCfg` in `ui.js`): `ornaments = min(6, L-1)`, `ornActive = 1 + floor((L-3)/2)`, `decor = L-3`; on 4 tiers this equals the official rule. `newGame` options: `ornaments`, `ornActive`, `ornWin`, `decor`.

## Online rooms

- V2 invitation: `?room=b2.<random-8-character-nonce>.<host-pubkey-hex>.<signed-create-event-id-hex>`. Both hex identifiers must have exactly 64 lowercase characters. The relay tag is `["t","boop-augmented-<nonce>"]`, kind 8812, JSON `v: 2`. Replay requires the full invitation and only accepts its exact create ID, host and room tag. Browser delivery explicitly verifies signatures; the pure replay helper expects already verified events.
- `create {cfg}` by the host (Snowball). All later events include `root: <create-event-id>`. `join` requests a seat; only a host-signed `admit {join:<request-id>, guest:<request-pubkey>}` grants Coal. The host waits for initial history EOSE, then auto-admits a pending request if no admission exists. With multiple pending requests at that decision, timestamp/ID order breaks ties. Later outsider requests cannot replace an admitted guest. A host must be online for initial admission, not for an already admitted guest to resume.
- Compatibility intentionally fails closed. Legacy nonce-only codes and v1 histories cannot establish a trustworthy host/create binding and are not auto-upgraded, replayed or joined. The UI explains that the host must create a new room and send its full invitation. All participants must use the v2 client. Local saves and browser identity keys retain their existing storage formats.
- `move {seq, a}` uses `a = {k: orn|play|line|promote|ghost|skip, i}` with an additional boolean `c` required only for `play` and optional `play` fields `x` (`w`/`s`/`a`/`b`), `g` (0-2) and `d` (0-7, only with `g: 2`), emitted in the canonical order `k, i, c, x, g, d`; `ghost` takes an entry stitch id as `i` and `skip` has no `i` (any extra field is rejected). Sequence/index values must be nonnegative safe integers, indices must be in range, and unknown action fields are rejected. Replay retains canonical actions and accepted event IDs, never coerced originals. Rules still decide legality and turn authority; for competing moves at one sequence, timestamp then ID determines the canonical event.
- UI animation requires an exact accepted-event prefix plus one new action, not just a larger sequence. Equal-length forks, changed admission identity, shortened histories, and multi-action updates replace the board atomically from replay. Pending updates drain after animation; action/application exceptions restore replay (or the previous local state) and release busy in `finally`.
- Security boundary: this prevents the reported outsider backdated-create/admission takeover when the authentic invitation is used. It does not authenticate who sent an invitation, reserve the open guest seat for a particular friend, protect a compromised host/browser key, stop an authorized player or host from signing conflicting moves/admissions, guarantee relay history completeness or availability, encrypt public events, or bound event-flood resources. Conflicting host admissions are deterministically ordered, not consensus-final. Do not claim general protection against malicious hosts or relays.
- Wire versions: `b2` (v2) is exactly the original 2-player tree room and refuses `shape`/`ghost` in its config. `b3` (v3) carries 2-4 players with per-seat host admission (`admit {join, guest, seat}`; no move counts until every seat is filled), `ornaments: 0`, and the square config `{shape:'square', size:6-10, pieces, players, ghost?:true}` (tree-only fields dropped, kittens must fit `maxPieces`). The square config also accepts `shuffle: true` (needs 6+ pieces, no `ghost`, `maxPieces` with the wild cells) and the tree config `boopmas: true`; each is a boolean, `false` is dropped, and `b2` refuses both keys. `Client.create` picks `b3` for anything that is not a plain 2-player tree (so also for `boopmas` and `shuffle`). Nothing was bumped: `b3` has not shipped yet, so the new keys are part of its first public shape; an older `b3` client would silently drop `boopmas`/`shuffle` and then reject the new action fields, so do not publish an older b3 build next to this one. Old clients cannot read `b3`; there is no negotiation. The replay is deterministic for ghosts because every ghost action goes through `Logic.placeGhost`/`skipGhost`, and ghost movement is a pure consequence of the piece move.
- Keys: one throwaway key per browser (`boop-augmented-nostr-key-v1` in localStorage); no login. Relays: damus, nos.lol, primal, snort; `?relays=wss://a,wss://b` overrides them (tests use a fake relay).
- Measured on real relays: room created in about 4 s, guest seated about 1 s later, a move delivered in 0.4 s.
- Not yet: rematch in the same room, chat, NIP-07 login, a public lobby.

## CPU

Ghost placement is separate: see `bestGhost` above. Powers: see the Boopmas and Shuffle sections above (spirits at the root of the search only; Shuffle pieces are plain candidates). Every full turn (ornament cell x placement, with line choice and promotion auto-resolved) is scored, then the top 10 are checked against the opponent's best reply (ornament cells sampled to 3). Eval: cats, captured ornaments, open pairs, ornaments exposed to a push off the tree. About 5 / 9 / 21 ms per move on 4 / 6 / 8 tiers.

## Motion and art

- Constants in `ui.js`, exposed as `__boop.motion`: `BOOP_MS = 1000` (was 560), `SCARE_MS = 1000`, `GHOST_STEP_MS = 800`, `PLACE_PAUSE_MS = 300` (was 140), easing `cubic-bezier(.45,0,.2,1)`; graduation, line and promotion waits were lengthened too. A scared piece that leaps hops through a mid keyframe (`S * 0.75` px up). Input stays gated (`busy`) until the last motion finishes. The CPU's thinking delay (520 ms) is unchanged.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` (read once at load): `wait()` returns immediately, no Web Animations are created, and the CSS disables remaining animations and transitions; the result is applied in one step.
- Cats: thick vector strokes (4-8 units on a 100-unit box), `geometricPrecision`, no `drop-shadow`/blur filters anywhere on pieces (2D, 3D or the grad pulse). The tree's 3D lighting is only a brightness step. Cats wear Santa hats on the tree (`data-art="festive"`) and bow ties on squares (`data-art="plain"`); the ghost cat is a separate SVG (`data-art="spirit"`).

### What the new tests do and do not prove

- Rules (`boopmas.test.js`, `shuffle.test.js`): hand-computed positions for every power, the refusals (bad `g`/`d`/`x`, spent spirit, wrong seat, scaredy cat, blankie on an empty or covered cell), saves (round trip plus 20+ hostile variants), and random-play fuzzing over 2-4 players and every size: per-seat piece, blankie and wild conservation, unique ids, save round-trips and replay-equals-play. The fuzz saw hundreds of each power and every branch counter above 5.
- Online (`online_powers.test.js`): config validation, canonical action forms, full-game replay with real room events (order and duplicate independence), wrong-seat and wrong-phase rejection, and the real `Client` with real signatures over an in-memory relay. `online_powers_browser_test.py` repeats this with real browsers through a fake WebSocket relay.
- Not covered: keyboard-only play is tested for aiming Meowly and the blankie only, not for tabbing through every control; no screen-reader pass; touch input beyond Playwright's phone viewport; no full-length CPU-vs-CPU game in the browser (the browser CPU tests play a handful of turns); blanket movement is shown as a cut, not a glide, and no test checks it visually; 3D and README screenshots were reviewed by eye. The Boopmas and Shuffle rules are checked against my reading of public descriptions, not against the printed rulebooks, the Shuffle cards or a physical copy, so wherever the README says "my choice" a physical copy may differ. No test or code contacts a real relay. The CPU is a heuristic: it was not tuned for the powers, only checked to move legally and to use them.
- Balance probe (`GAMES=8`, 1-ply CPU self-play, `SHAPE=square SHUFFLE=1` / `BOOPMAS=1`): Shuffle 6x6 with 8 kittens: 8/8 games ended, 37 plies on average (2 players), 101 (4 players); 8x8/12: 29; 10x10/16: 31; all won by cats. Boopmas on the 4-tier tree: 8 kittens 24 plies (6 nice, 2 naughty), 12 kittens 26; 6 tiers 25-33; 4 players 20 plies, all naughty (the 4-player naughty rate was not compared with spirits off). CPU decision time: slowest 29 ms with spirits (4 tiers) and 134 ms with Shuffle (10x10, 4 players) in Node.

Earlier gaps still apply: no test covers keyboard-only play of the ghost stitches (20-36 buttons), and the ghost rules are checked against my reading of the publisher's text, not a physical BOOoop set.
